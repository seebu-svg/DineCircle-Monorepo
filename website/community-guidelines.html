Community Guidelines

DineCircle is built around a table with strangers at it. These guidelines exist so the person booking from a phone can trust the room they arrive in.

Effective 7 November 2026

Who these guidelines apply to

These guidelines apply to everyone who uses DineCircle: diners browsing this website, guests who join a hosted experience, hosts and food creators who publish one, and the venues that publish menus, prices and offers. They apply to content you publish on DineCircle and to conduct at the table.

They are not a legal contract. Where they and the terms of service differ, the terms govern.

Hosting and attending with respect

A host sets the tone of a table. Guests choose it expecting what the listing described, so publish an honest description of the format, the crowd and what happens on the night.

  • Describe the experience as it will actually run, including whether it is a group table or a shared seating format.
  • No pressure to buy anything, recruit for anything, or take part in anything beyond the meal.
  • Contact details of guests belong to those guests. Do not collect, share or sell them.
  • Treat venue staff as partners, not service to be directed. The venue owns the room.

Guests have a matching duty: arrive roughly when you said you would, tell the host early if you cannot come so the seat can go to someone else, and behave towards the other people at the table the way you would want a stranger to behave towards you.

Venue listings and content

Partners publish their own information, and diners rely on it when deciding where to spend an evening.

  • Menus, prices, opening hours and amenities must be current and must be yours to publish.
  • Photographs must show the actual food, room or team, not stock imagery standing in for it.
  • Deal terms must state what the offer applies to, when it runs and what it cannot be combined with.
  • A verified badge means we reviewed the partnership details. It is not a quality rating and we do not adjust it for a subscription size.

Reviews, and no manipulation

Reviews are most useful when they are honest and specific.

  • Only review somewhere you actually ate or joined.
  • No paid, swapped, bulk or competitor-suppressed reviews, and no reviews of your own venue written as a guest.
  • No personal attacks, slurs or published contact details of staff or guests. Criticise the meal and the service, not the person.
  • Hosts and venues may reply. A reply is welcome; removing or reporting an honest review to make it disappear is not.

Experience safety and cancellation etiquette

DineCircle does not take payment, so the money relationship is always between a guest and a venue. Nothing on this platform is a promise of money back, and no host should present it as one.

  • Agree the menu, the seating and the cancellation notice with the venue before you publish.
  • If a date or a format has to move, tell joined guests as early as you can rather than at the door.
  • A guest who cancels late takes a seat someone else could have had. Tell the host, do not simply not turn up.
  • Anything that feels unsafe is not a community question. Contact the relevant authorities first, then tell us so we can act on the listing.

Reporting and how we act

Report a listing, a review or a host by emailing support@dinecircle.app with the link to the page and what happened. Reports are read by a person, and we keep the reporter's details out of any message to the other side.

We act in steps, and the step depends on the finding rather than on how many times a page has been reported:

  1. A conversation. For unclear or first-time content, we contact the person who published it and ask for a change.
  2. Content hidden or edited. A review, a listing claim or a photo that breaks these guidelines can be unpublished while it is fixed.
  3. Access limited. Repeated or deliberate abuse can cost the ability to post reviews, publish listings or host experiences.
  4. Accounts closed. Fake listings, review manipulation, harvesting guest contact details, or conduct that puts guests or staff at risk ends the account. Where a subscription is paid, closure for these reasons does not create a refund.

Partners can ask us to review a decision, and we will explain the finding. Venues that repeatedly publish prices or hours that do not match the table lose the listing rather than keep editing it.
